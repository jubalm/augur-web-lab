Augur homepage: a clear, visual wireframe

HIGHEST PRIORITY: VISIBLE DENSITY AND FIRST-SCREEN CONTRACT

This long brief is instruction and background, NOT webpage copy. Do not render its background paragraphs, source inventory, or review notes across the homepage. Do not copy technical caveats wholesale; preserve concise, decision-critical qualifications through truthful labels and copy, without production-disclaimer noise. Create a simple page people understand by scanning clear typography, composed boxes, diagrams, and a few user-controlled states.

• Default homepage copy, including the hero: aim for 300–400 words; hard ceiling 450, excluding navigation/footer and content hidden behind interaction. Shorter is welcome when meaning survives. Hero: maximum 65 words excluding navigation. These are project constraints, not scientific UX rules.
• Usually give a section one heading and one short supporting line. Cards get a label and, when needed, one short sentence. One main idea per module. If essential accuracy needs more explanation, redesign the module or route readers to detail; do not shrink type.
• Show ONE active mechanism explanation and ONE active application at a time. Other states belong in separately linked state frames, never as repeated expanded modules down the default homepage.
• Viewport 1 contains NO query form, question card, report interface, staking/bond controls, simulated dashboard, or visitor guessing exercise. Use the chosen concise hero copy, two CTAs, and a considered animation placeholder composed as real artwork. This explicitly supersedes the earlier interactive hero/example requirement.
• No visitor-facing production commentary such as “for display purposes only,” “not real,” “AI generated,” “placeholder,” or “animation goes here.” Keep authoring instructions outside homepage frames. Avoid misleading content in the first place; use truthful editorial framing such as “Possible applications” where qualification matters.
• The result must feel SIMPLE and CLEAR. More visible information is not a better deliverable. The prompt’s depth should become design judgment, not interface density.
• Lean slightly minimalist: clean spacing, fewer competing elements, and purposeful detail. Keep Augur’s visual identity, diagram richness, and useful interactions. This is light restraint, not a sterile aesthetic: avoid excessive blank space, tiny text, stripped-out meaning, or reducing every section to the same plain boxes.

Workflow: quality takes priority over speed; do not rush to the first draft. Before designing, research authoritative UX/UI guidance, visually inspect the actual reference and inspiration sites, and test relevant interactions where possible. Distinguish observed behavior from inference; never invent observations. Explore two or three distinct visual directions before building, then choose the strongest, most Augur-specific direction. Avoid generic web3 templates, interchangeable section patterns, and decorative novelty without meaning. Build deliberately, then critique rendered screenshots for composition, hierarchy, density, typography, visual specificity, and interaction clarity. Review the result against the actual inspected web-lab requirements and refine weak areas. Repeat focused inspection and repair until the measurable acceptance criteria below pass or a concrete blocker is documented. Deliver finished work rather than promises of future refinement; do not loop indefinitely.

4. Narrative: communicate visually, disclose deliberately

Keep the macro sequence: hero → principles → mechanism → applications → roadmap/development → blog → FAQ → contact/footer. Supporting sections may be compact or visually grouped. Do not turn eight purposes into eight similarly sized blocks of paragraphs.

Hero
Compose the chosen concise hero copy, CTAs, and one distinctive visual. Evaluate first-screen framing at 1440 × 900. The visitor should understand the positioning without touching anything. Never move query/report/staking UI into the hero to satisfy a desire for interactivity.

Build a representative animation keyframe from native shapes: a carefully composed symbol, field, or relationship between independent elements. Give it a strong, inspectable focal point. The visual can evoke independent participation and shared resolution without pretending to show live consensus or a real transaction. Avoid a generic mesh, spinning token, or oversized empty box. Draw a separate two-to-three-keyframe strip showing the intended animation. Keep timing, production method, and replacement criteria in adjacent notes, never centered inside the artwork.

Principles
Use a small, differentiated set of visual statements. Each should reveal a useful property of the system rather than restating the hero. Try an editorial bento with deliberate differences in scale and emphasis, or another legible composition. Static boxes are welcome when clear. Mix static and interactive modules deliberately; never make every section interactive or turn boxes into miniature essays. Make labels and visual relationships do the work.

Mechanism
Give the visitor a persistent, compact overview of asking, reporting, possible challenge, and finalization. Distinguish the optional challenge loop from the forward sequence. Keep the essential premise visible: an answer is tentative during its appeal period; an unchallenged close makes the latest report final.

Below or beside that overview, show one selected stage with one concise explanation and one editable visual. Selecting another stage replaces this panel. Provide an obvious selected state and a direct paper/deeper-explanation route. A challenged state should visibly replace the tentative answer and restart the appeal period. Do not require every technical detail to appear on the homepage. Put fork mechanics and advanced conditions in dedicated linked detail states or the paper, preserving accurate meaning.

Applications
Use one user-selected context at a time: market milestone by a deadline; flight insurance; grant work. Make the selection replace a single visual panel. Show a concrete question → answer → application action relationship using minimal labels and boxes. The application applies its own settlement, policy, or grant terms. “Possible applications” establishes context without repeated warnings.

Do not show fabricated customer dashboards, live-looking feeds, payouts, volume, yields, or integration logos. An illustrative schematic should use conceptual entities, not invented operational data. Do not include a hero-style “Try it” question or ask visitors to guess facts without evidence. Use selection instead of mock transactions.

Development, blog, FAQ, contact
Make development a compact, honest orientation and a route to follow work. Use dated verified status when available; do not imply that September progress is current October progress. If status cannot be verified, omit unsupported milestones and use a durable development link. No public “pending confirmation” production placeholders.

Blog is a quiet gateway: a small number of verified titles, restrained artwork, and a route onward. No invented posts, authors, or dates. FAQ contains residual questions, with answers collapsed by default and expanded states elsewhere. Never hide the essential proposition or material qualification there. Contact/footer prioritize reading, following development, and joining the conversation. Do not invent contact details, response promises, forms, subscriptions, wallet flows, or token-purchase actions.

5. Accuracy without an encyclopedia

Augur is broader decentralized oracle infrastructure, not itself a prediction market. Use the full name “Augur Lituus” for the next-generation proposed oracle mechanism. Do not portray proposed architecture as a launched service. No invented partners, users, metrics, audits, supported chains, SDK availability, yields, dates, testimonials, security guarantees, or guarantees of truth.

Keep these facts available for accurate diagrams and deeper states, not as a checklist of visible homepage paragraphs:
• An application supplies a question, mutually exclusive outcomes, resolution criteria, and a REP fee. An initial reporter bonds a tentative outcome.
• A challenger bonds a different outcome, ordinarily doubling the previous bond, with special handling near the fork threshold. A completed challenge replaces the tentative answer and restarts the appeal period. The largest bond does not prove truth or finality. Losing bonds are at risk. The initial reporting deadline and appeal period differ.
• If the appeal period closes without a completed challenge, the latest report becomes final. Invalid covers malformed or unresolvable questions.
• A fork creates outcome universes with one-way REP migration, supply-restoration minting/auction, and further migration. The original oracle follows the outcome with most total migration. Applications apply their own payout rules. Do not simplify this to “highest stake wins” or invent fork arithmetic, timings, or attack costs.

Avoid unnecessary ecosystem names. If needed: Zoltar is a forkable foundation sharing REP, not a truth-deciding authority; Augur Stratoblast is Dark Florist’s prediction markets on Zoltar; Lituus Foundation coordinates funding and review. September sources describe ChainSafe implementation with testing, audits, and documentation ahead. Moon Fork concerns Augur v2, not a Lituus launch. Do not reproduce the erroneous Augur v2 “2018” chronology.

7. Desktop clarity and accessibility

No essential hover dependency.

Specify logical reading/focus order, visible focus, descriptive controls, and semantic headings. Tabs need an obvious selected state and associated panel; arrow keys move between tabs, with Enter/Space for manual activation. Automatic activation requires imperceptible latency. The default panel must explain its premise without a click; accordions need named buttons and expanded/collapsed state. Use more than color to identify a selected, tentative, challenged, or final state. Give complex visuals concise accessible equivalents preserving their relationships.

Target WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 ordinary text, 3:1 large text and relevant non-text controls/graphics. Project 48/44 px controls exceed many minimum requirements; do not mislabel 44 px as universal AA. Automatically moving content exceeding five seconds alongside other content needs pause/stop/hide unless essential. Reduced-motion alternatives are project practice; do not claim static Figma inspection certifies accessibility.
